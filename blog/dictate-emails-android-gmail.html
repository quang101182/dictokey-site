Dictate Emails on Android: Gmail and Outlook by Voice

Published November 11, 2026 · DictoKey Team · 6 min read

Short answer. To dictate an email on Android, open a new message in Gmail or Outlook, tap the body and use the microphone on your keyboard: Gboard has one built in. If you want a floating bubble that adds punctuation and capital letters for you and works over the keyboard you already use, DictoKey does that, but it needs an internet connection. The method is the same in any mail app because dictation happens in the keyboard layer, not in the mail app.

Email is where dictation pays off most: replies are long, the sentences are complete, and you can read the result once before sending. This page compares three ways to do it on Android, using Google help pages checked on November 11, 2026. We make DictoKey, so we also say where the others fit better.

Start with what Gmail itself offers

We did not find a Google help page describing a dedicated dictation button inside the Gmail app, so we do not claim one exists. What we did find is Google’s keyboard help: voice typing is a feature of the keyboard, which means it works in a Gmail or Outlook compose window the same way it works in a notes app. A third-party article we saw in search mentioned a Gmail drafting feature that accepts voice prompts, but it is tied to specific accounts and plans and it writes a draft for you rather than transcribing your words, so we leave it out of the comparison.

Option 1: the Gboard microphone

According to Google’s help page, you open an app, tap a text field, tap the microphone at the top of the keyboard and, when you see “Speak now”, say what you want written. Say period, comma, question mark, exclamation point, new line or new paragraph to insert punctuation, though these phrases may not be available in every language. To replace a word, touch and hold it, tap the microphone and say the new word. Google also notes that talk-to-text does not work with all languages. The page does not say whether it needs an internet connection.

For a quick two-line reply this is the easiest start: nothing to install, and you keep the keyboard you know.

Option 2: advanced voice typing on a Pixel

Google describes an upgraded mode, called advanced voice typing, with stricter requirements: a Pixel 6 or later, including Fold and Tablet models. Punctuation is added automatically as you speak, and you can turn that off under Settings > Voice typing > Auto punctuation. Google says your spoken text stays on your device, except when you use Fix it or detailed edits, which send it to Google servers. Fix it requires a Pixel 8 or later and detailed edits a Pixel 8 or later. If you own a supported Pixel, this is a strong free option, and you should try it before anything else.

Option 3: a dictation app with a floating bubble

DictoKey gives you two independent ways to dictate: a standard Android keyboard, and a floating bubble above any app. If one is not available, the other still works. In Gmail or Outlook, you can keep your usual keyboard and tap the bubble to speak. It is a two-part pill: language on one side, microphone on the other. Tap the microphone to dictate; a long press dictates and then translates (adjustable).

Clean mode restores punctuation and capital letters automatically, so a dictated paragraph rarely needs retouching. You can drag the bubble onto Translate, Correct or Emojis to rework what is already in the field, and in the keyboard a long press on the dictation button lets you say the correction you want. For emails in two languages, DictoKey supports 51 languages with auto-detect and real-time translation.

A routine for dictating an email

  1. Fill in the recipient and subject by typing; names and addresses are the part dictation handles least well.
  2. Tap the body, start dictating and speak one paragraph at a time, then pause.
  3. Say the greeting and sign-off too, so the message reads as one piece.
  4. Read the text once before sending, checking names, numbers and dates.
  5. Fix a single phrase by voice correction or the Correct action instead of retyping the paragraph.

Dictating the first draft and editing by hand afterwards is often faster than typing everything, and it reduces the amount of typing for people with sore hands or wrists.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I dictate an email in the Gmail app on Android?

Yes, with a keyboard or dictation app rather than a Gmail button. Open a new message, tap the body, then tap the microphone on Gboard (or the bubble or keyboard dictation button in DictoKey) and speak. The same method works in the Outlook app, because it relies on the keyboard and not on the mail app.

How do I add punctuation when dictating an email?

With Gboard you can say phrases such as period, comma, new line or new paragraph, though availability varies by language. Google says that on Pixel 6 and later with advanced voice typing, punctuation is added automatically. DictoKey's Clean mode adds punctuation and capital letters automatically.

Is dictating emails free on Android?

The Gboard microphone is built into the keyboard and we did not find a price on Google's help page. DictoKey has a free plan of 100 dictations a day in your first week, then 30 a day, with no account needed.

Does DictoKey need internet to dictate an email?

Yes. DictoKey transcribes in the cloud and needs an internet connection. Google says advanced voice typing on supported Pixel phones keeps your spoken text on the device.

Can I write the email in another language?

DictoKey supports 51 languages with auto-detect and real-time translation, so you can speak in one language and send text in another. Gboard's help page says voice typing does not work with all languages.

What if I lose the dictation when switching apps to check a detail?

DictoKey keeps a dictation that could not be inserted for a few minutes, in memory only, and offers to insert it in the next field you tap. It never inserts automatically.
