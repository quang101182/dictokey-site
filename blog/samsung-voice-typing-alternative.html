Samsung Voice Typing Alternative for Galaxy Phones

Published October 13, 2026 · DictoKey Team · 6 min read

Short answer. Every Galaxy phone already ships with two voice typing engines: Samsung voice input and Google Voice Typing, and you can switch between them in Settings in under a minute. If neither fits, DictoKey adds a voice keyboard and a floating dictation bubble that works next to the Samsung Keyboard, restores punctuation automatically, and can rewrite or translate what you said, in 52 languages, without an account.

Samsung voice input is the microphone on the Samsung Keyboard toolbar. It is built in, it can use downloadable offline language packs, and for many people it is all they need. But it is tied to that keyboard: Samsung's own support page states that Samsung voice input is only available when using the Samsung Keyboard. If you want a different keyboard, more punctuation help, rewriting, or a way to dictate without opening the keyboard at all, you need something else.

This page is about replacing or complementing Samsung's voice input. If you are looking for Galaxy AI writing tools or for what AI dictation exists on each phone brand, read our separate guide on AI voice dictation on Samsung and other Android phones.

Step 1: try the free switch Samsung already gives you

Before installing anything, it is worth testing Google Voice Typing, which Samsung lists as the second built-in voice-to-text option on Galaxy phones. The steps from Samsung's support pages:

  1. Open Settings, tap General management, then Keyboard list and default.
  2. Turn on the voice input you want. Samsung advises keeping only one voice assistant active.
  3. In Samsung Keyboard settings, tap Voice input and select which engine the keyboard microphone should use.
  4. To dictate, touch the Microphone icon on the keyboard toolbar and speak.

Samsung notes that the exact menus vary by region and software version. In the same settings, the Offline voice input section lets you download language packs for Samsung voice input.

Why people look beyond Samsung voice input

The documented limits explain most of the reasons:

  • It is locked to one keyboard. Prefer Gboard, SwiftKey or another layout for typing, and Samsung voice input is no longer available.
  • You have to open the keyboard first. Dictation starts from a toolbar icon, which means finding and hitting a small target every time. For people with tremors or limited hand mobility, that extra precise tap is the hard part.
  • It types what you say, nothing more. Restoring punctuation, tidying a rambling message or translating it are separate jobs.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I change the voice input on a Samsung Galaxy phone?

Samsung's support pages describe two built-in voice-to-text options on Galaxy phones: Samsung voice input and Google Voice Typing. Open Settings, tap General management, then Keyboard list and default, and turn on the one you want. In Samsung Keyboard settings, the Voice input option lets you choose which one the keyboard microphone uses. Menu names can vary by region and software version.

Does Samsung voice input work without the Samsung Keyboard?

No. Samsung's US support page states that Samsung voice input is only available when using the Samsung Keyboard. If you switch to another keyboard, you use that keyboard's voice typing instead, or an app such as DictoKey that adds its own voice keyboard and floating bubble.

Can I dictate offline on a Samsung phone?

Samsung voice input can use offline language packs, which you download from the Offline voice input section of its settings. DictoKey does not work offline: it transcribes speech in the cloud and needs an internet connection. Wispr Flow's Android documentation also says a connection is required.
