Yaps Alternative for Android: What to Use Instead in 2026

People look for a Yaps alternative for ordinary reasons: they want to keep their usual keyboard, they need a language or feature that Yaps does not list, or they prefer a different way of triggering dictation. This page compares the options as we found them on official pages on November 5, 2026. We make DictoKey, so we also list the strengths of the others.

What Yaps is, from its own pages

Yaps launched its Android app in April 2026 as an AI keyboard with voice dictation built in. Its Android page says you press or hold the Yaps button on the keyboard to dictate, that dictation, notes and read-aloud run on your phone, and that they work without internet once local models are installed. It also says Android is free forever for voice typing, voice notes with local search and read-aloud, requires no account, and needs Android 10 or newer. Its Pro plan lists desktop apps for Windows, macOS and Linux and costs $12 per month billed annually ($144 per year) or $15 per month, with a 7-day free trial.

Those are real strengths: a free Android plan with no usage cap listed, no account, and (by Yaps's own description) processing that stays on the phone. If that is your priority, Yaps is worth a close look.

Why people switch

  • Keeping your keyboard. If you like Gboard, Samsung Keyboard or SwiftKey for typing, a floating bubble lets you dictate without swapping keyboards. DictoKey has two independent ways to dictate, a standard Android voice keyboard and the bubble, so if one is unavailable the other still works.
  • Reworking text you already wrote. Drag the DictoKey bubble onto Translate, Correct or Emojis to rework what is in the field, and step back with Previous / Next.
  • Translation and a second language. DictoKey translates in real time, offers automatic language detection, and has an Interpreter Mode for spoken conversations between two languages. Free users can try the Interpreter 3 times a day.
  • Mistakes while speaking. Clean mode restores punctuation and capital letters automatically, and in the keyboard a long press on the dictation button lets you say the correction.
  • Less typing for tired hands. The bubble can be resized, made more or less opaque and anchored where your thumb reaches, which can help if long typing sessions are uncomfortable.

What DictoKey does not do

It needs an internet connection, so it will not help on a plane without Wi-Fi; if working without a connection matters most to you, Yaps fits better. It supports 51 languages. It runs on Android 10 and later and on Windows 10/11 with a separate subscription, but not on iOS or Mac. It also blocks banking apps, password managers and crypto wallets by design, so it works in most apps rather than all of them.

Who should pick what

  • Pick Yaps if you want a free Android keyboard that works without internet, no account, and notes and read-aloud in the same app.
  • Pick Gboard voice typing if free and already installed is enough.
  • Pick Wispr Flow if you prefer its rewriting-first approach and also dictate on a computer.
  • Pick DictoKey if you want to keep your current keyboard, dictate from a bubble you can resize and anchor, translate or rework text by dragging, and start without an account. It is also a good fit for people who want to cut down on typing because of hand pain or limited dexterity, as described on our accessibility page.
