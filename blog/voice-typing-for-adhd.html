Voice Typing for ADHD on Android: Capture Thoughts Fast

Published November 12, 2026 · DictoKey Team · 6 min read

Short answer. If a thought disappears while you hunt for the right app or keyboard, speaking it is usually the quickest way to catch it. On Android you can use the Gboard microphone for free, Google’s Voice Access to control the phone by voice, or a floating bubble such as DictoKey that sits above your apps and adds punctuation for you. DictoKey needs an internet connection. None of these is a treatment for ADHD: they are writing tools.

People who struggle to hold a thought while they unlock a phone, open a notes app and start typing often prefer to talk first and edit later. What a speed gap looks like in practice

The NHS describes inattentive signs in children such as being easily distracted and forgetting everyday tasks, and hyperactive or impulsive signs such as interrupting conversations. Its page covers children and young people and does not mention dictation, so we use it only for context. The practical point is narrower: every extra step between a thought and a saved note is a chance to lose the thought. Fewer steps help, and speaking removes the slowest one, which is typing.

Dictation will not organise your day for you, and it does not remove the need to reread what you wrote. What it can do is lower the effort of the first draft, a quick reminder or a reply you would otherwise postpone.

Option 1: the Gboard microphone

According to Google’s help page, you tap a text field, tap the microphone at the top of the keyboard and, when you see “Speak now”, say what you want written. You can say period, comma, exclamation point, question mark, new line or new paragraph to add punctuation, although punctuation might not be available in all languages. Google also says talk-to-text does not work with all languages. The page does not say whether an internet connection is needed or what it costs.

If you already use Gboard, this is the lowest-friction place to start: nothing to install.

Option 2: Voice Access

Voice Access is a different kind of tool. Google describes it as letting you control your device with spoken commands: open apps, navigate and edit text hands-free. You start it from a floating shortcut button, or by saying “Hey Google, start Voice Access” when that detection is on, and you can say “Open Gmail” as a command. It works in English, Spanish, German, Italian, French, Portuguese or Japanese. For use without a network connection you need to download language packs for offline speech recognition. It suits you if you want to drive the whole phone by voice, not only write.

Option 3: a floating dictation bubble

DictoKey gives you two independent ways to dictate: a standard Android keyboard, and a floating bubble above any app. If one is not available, the other still works. The bubble is a two-part pill with language on one side and microphone on the other. Tap the microphone to dictate; a long press dictates and then translates (adjustable).

Clean mode restores punctuation and capital letters automatically, so a spoken stream of thoughts comes out as readable sentences. You can drag the bubble onto Translate, Correct or Emojis to rework text already in the field, and you can size the bubble, set its opacity and choose its position.

One feature matters for interrupted days: if a dictation cannot be inserted, for example because the keyboard was lowered or you left the field, DictoKey keeps the text for a few minutes in memory only and offers an Insert chip at the next field you tap. It is not saved for later, and it never inserts by itself.

Habits that make dictation stick

  1. Put the dictation button where your thumb already is, and keep a notes app one tap away.
  2. Speak one thought at a time, then pause; short bursts are easier to review than one long monologue.
  3. Say it first, fix it later: do not edit while you speak.
  4. Reread once before sending, checking names, numbers and dates.
  5. Fix a phrase by voice or with the Correct action instead of retyping the whole message.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can voice typing help with ADHD?

Speaking is often quicker than typing, so dictation can help you get a thought down before it slips away. It is a writing tool, not a treatment, and it does not replace advice from a health professional. Many people with ADHD find it easier to talk out a first draft and tidy it afterwards.

What is the fastest way to capture a thought on Android?

Keep a notes app open or one tap away, then use a voice keyboard or floating bubble to speak it. In DictoKey the bubble sits above your apps, so you can start dictating without switching keyboards. Gboard's microphone is built into the keyboard.

Will punctuation be added for me?

With Gboard you say phrases such as period, comma or new paragraph, though Google notes punctuation might not be available in all languages. DictoKey's Clean mode adds punctuation and capital letters automatically, so a dictated message rarely needs retouching.

Does DictoKey need internet?

Yes. DictoKey transcribes in the cloud and needs a connection. Google says Voice Access can work offline if you download language packs for offline speech recognition.

What if I lose a dictation because I switched apps?

DictoKey keeps a dictation that could not be inserted for a few minutes, in memory only, and shows an Insert chip at the next field you tap. It never inserts automatically, and never in password fields or sensitive apps.

Is it free?

The Gboard microphone is built into the keyboard and we did not find a price on Google's help page. DictoKey has a free plan: 100 dictations a day in your first week, then 30 a day, with no account. Premium is about $7.50/month or about $55 per year, with a 7-day free trial.
