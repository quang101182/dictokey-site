Voice Dictation Button for Any Keyboard on Android: Keep Yours, Add a Mic

Published October 23, 2026 · DictoKey Team · 6 min read

Short answer. To keep Gboard, Samsung Keyboard or SwiftKey and still get a better dictation button, use a floating bubble that sits above your apps instead of a keyboard that replaces yours. Wispr Flow and DictoKey both offer one on Android. DictoKey's bubble also lets you drag it onto Translate, Correct or Emojis to rework text you already wrote.

→ Get DictoKey on Google Play (free plan: 30 dictations per day; internet connection required).

Many people like their typing keyboard and only want a faster way to speak. The built-in microphone key is fine for a quick sentence, but it lives on the keyboard, so you have to reach it, and it does little beyond writing what it hears. Here is how the main approaches differ, using vendor help pages checked today.

Approach 1: the microphone key on your keyboard

Google's help page says to tap a text field, tap the microphone at the top of Gboard and speak when you see “Speak now”. It needs nothing extra, and your keyboard stays exactly as it is. The trade-off is that it only exists while that keyboard is open, and Google notes that voice typing does not work with every language. If it misbehaves, see our Gboard troubleshooting guide or the Samsung equivalent.

Approach 2: a voice keyboard that replaces yours

Typeless is described by its listing as an AI voice keyboard that replaces your default keyboard, with filler-word cleanup and a personal dictionary. That can work well if you are happy to dictate most of the time, but you switch keyboards to use it, and switching back to type is an extra step. We could not confirm its Android pricing or free limits today, so the table shows a dash.

Approach 3: a floating bubble above your apps

A bubble is separate from the keyboard. Wispr Flow's help page says you tap a text field while the on-screen keyboard is visible and the bubble appears; it hides when the keyboard is dismissed. It needs Microphone, Accessibility Service and “Display over other apps”, an online device, and it is hidden in restricted banking apps and in password, numeric and phone fields. The page does not say which keyboards it supports, so check that before you commit.

DictoKey takes the same idea further. Its floating bubble is independent from its own voice keyboard, so if one is not available the other still works, and you can keep your usual keyboard for typing. The bubble is a two-part pill: the microphone half dictates (a long press dictates and then translates), and the language half opens a menu for dictation and translation languages, style, Emojis, copying your last dictation, Interpreter and translating the clipboard. Size, opacity and position are adjustable, and you can enable it app by app.

Why the drag panel matters

Dictation rarely comes out perfect on the first pass. With DictoKey you drag the bubble and a panel appears with Previous, Translate, Correct, Emojis and Next. Drop the bubble on one and it acts on the text already in the field; Previous and Next step back and forward like on the keyboard. In practice that lets you keep typing on your own keyboard, dictate a long passage, then fix it without opening another app. Clean mode also restores punctuation and capital letters automatically.

The honest limits: transcription is in the cloud, so you need a connection; the free plan is capped at 30 dictations per day; it works in most apps but banks, password managers and crypto wallets are blocked automatically. It is GDPR compliant, stores no recordings and shows no ads. It coexists with TalkBack, and the accessibility page explains the permission in detail.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I keep Gboard or Samsung Keyboard and still use another dictation app?

Yes, if the dictation app uses a floating button instead of replacing your keyboard. DictoKey's bubble floats above the app you are using, so your typing keyboard stays as it is. Some apps, such as Typeless, are voice keyboards that replace your default keyboard instead.

What permissions does a floating dictation button need?

Wispr Flow's help page lists Microphone, Accessibility Service and Display over other apps. DictoKey's Accessibility permission shows the bubble, inserts your dictated text and reads the active field on your phone (for Previous / Next and spacing); that text is sent only when you drop the bubble on Translate, Correct or Emojis, and it is not stored.

Does a floating dictation button need internet?

Yes, for the apps compared here. Wispr Flow's help page says a device without a connection cannot start dictation, and DictoKey transcribes in the cloud, so it needs an internet connection.

What can I do with DictoKey's bubble besides dictating?

Tap the microphone half to dictate; a long press dictates and then translates. Tap the language half for a menu with languages, style, Emojis, copying your last dictation, Interpreter and translating the clipboard. Dragging the bubble onto Translate, Correct or Emojis reworks what you already wrote.

Is DictoKey free?

There is a free plan with a daily limit of 30 dictations and no account is needed. Premium costs about $5 per month or about $35 per year, with a 7-day free trial for new subscribers.

Does the bubble work in most apps?

It works in most apps, but banking apps, password managers and crypto wallets are blocked automatically. Wispr Flow similarly hides its bubble in restricted banking apps and password fields.
