For many people with dyslexia, the hard part of writing on a phone is not having ideas, it is spelling, tiny keys and the effort of rereading what was typed. Speaking a sentence can sidestep part of that. Dictation is not a treatment or a replacement for other support, and it does not suit everybody equally, but it can reduce how much you have to type and spell. An older overview from a university dyslexia resource says speech recognition can help people who find typing or writing difficult, while noting that dictation is not perfect (around one error every twenty words in the figure it quotes, from 2016, so treat that as a rough guide only).

Start with what Android already gives you

Two free tools are worth testing before you install anything:

  • Gboard voice typing. Open any text field, tap the microphone at the top of the keyboard, wait for “Speak now” and talk. Google's help page says you can talk to write in most places where you can type. You add punctuation by saying it (“period”, “comma”, “question mark”, “new line”, “new paragraph”), and you can replace a word by touching and holding it, tapping the microphone and saying the new word.
  • Voice Access. Google's accessibility app lets you open apps, navigate and edit text by voice. It can be turned on in Settings, then Accessibility, with a floating button that starts and stops listening. Google lists support for English, Spanish, German, Italian, French, Portuguese and Japanese, and optional offline language packs.

If you live in one of those languages and are happy to speak punctuation, these two cost nothing and may be all you need.

Features that tend to matter with dyslexia

1. Not having to dictate punctuation

Saying “comma” mid-sentence means thinking about the sentence and its punctuation at the same time. DictoKey's Clean mode restores punctuation and capital letters automatically, so you can speak the way you would to a friend. Gboard asks you to say the marks, which some people prefer because they stay in control.

2. Tidying up after you speak

Spoken thoughts ramble. DictoKey can rewrite dictated text with AI and translate it in real time, which helps when you want a message to read more clearly or in another language. Read the result before you send it: no tool is right every time, and names or unusual words may need a correction.

3. A way to dictate without hunting for a small target

DictoKey gives you two independent ways to dictate: an Android voice keyboard, and a floating bubble that sits above most apps. The bubble is adjustable in size, opacity and position and can be enabled app by app. If one way is unavailable, the other still works. Banking apps, password managers and crypto wallets are blocked automatically, and the Accessibility permission shows the bubble, inserts dictated text and reads the active field on the phone (for Previous / Next and spacing); that text is sent only when you choose Translate, Correct or Emojis, and it is not stored.

4. Privacy basics

DictoKey is GDPR compliant, stores no recordings and shows no ads. Speech is transcribed in the cloud, which is why a connection is needed and why it will not suit someone who must dictate offline.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can voice dictation help with dyslexia on Android?

For many people with dyslexia, speaking a sentence is easier than spelling and typing it, so dictation can reduce the amount of typing and spelling you have to do. It does not work equally well for everyone, and dictated text still needs a quick read-through, because speech recognition makes mistakes too. Try a free option first and see whether it suits the way you write.

Is Gboard voice typing good enough for dyslexia?

It can be. Gboard voice typing is built into Android keyboards, and Google's help page says you can talk to write in most places where you can type. Punctuation is spoken ("period", "comma", "new paragraph"), which some people find easy and others find distracting while they are trying to hold a thought.

Does DictoKey add punctuation automatically?

Yes. DictoKey's Clean mode restores punctuation and capital letters automatically, so you can speak naturally instead of saying each punctuation mark. You can also ask the AI to rewrite the dictated text, for example to make it clearer. Always read the result before sending anything important.

Does DictoKey work without an internet connection?

No. DictoKey transcribes speech in the cloud, so it needs an internet connection. If you need to dictate without a connection, look at tools that offer offline language packs, such as Voice Access on recent Android versions.

Is DictoKey free, and do I need an account?

You do not need an account to start. The free plan has a daily limit of 30 dictations at the time of writing. Premium costs about $7.50 per month or less on the annual plan, with a 7-day free trial for new subscribers. Google Play shows the exact price in your local currency.

Is DictoKey a medical or learning-support tool?

No. DictoKey is a voice-to-text accessibility tool. It can help reduce typing and spelling effort, but it does not diagnose, treat or teach anything. Dyslexia support is best built from several tools and, where relevant, advice from your school, employer or an educational specialist.
