Typing With One Finger on Android: Glide, Voice and Other Options

Published October 22, 2026 · DictoKey Team · 6 min read

Short answer. To type on Android with one finger or one hand, turn on Gboard's glide typing, where you slide a single finger across the letters and lift it at the end of each word. To type even less, dictate: Gboard has a microphone key, and DictoKey adds a voice keyboard plus a movable floating bubble, with Clean mode adding punctuation automatically.

Typing with one finger is common after an injury, with a hand condition, while holding a bag or a child, or simply because a large phone is hard to reach across. The good news is that Android already has several ways to cut down on tapping. This page goes through them from lightest to most hands-free, using Google help pages, and says where each one stops being enough.

Option 1: glide typing in Gboard

Google's help page explains that you slide your finger across the letters of a word, then lift it. To turn it on, open Settings > System > Languages & input > Virtual keyboard > Gboard and choose Glide typing. To replace a word, tap it and pick a suggestion; to remove one, double-tap it and tap Delete.

The limits are worth knowing: some steps need Android 8.0 or later, not all languages work with glide typing, and on a custom Android version the steps may differ, in which case Google suggests checking with your device manufacturer. You still need one accurate finger path per word, which can be tiring if your hand shakes or hurts.

Option 2: the microphone on your keyboard

Gboard's help page says to tap a text field, tap the microphone at the top of the keyboard and, when you see “Speak now”, say what you want written. You can say punctuation words such as period, comma, question mark or new line, though punctuation phrases may not exist in every language. To fix one word, touch and hold it, tap the microphone and say the replacement. Talk-to-text does not work with all languages, and some Pixel phones use a separate “Advanced voice typing” feature. If it misbehaves, see our Gboard voice typing troubleshooting guide.

Option 3: Voice Access for controlling the phone

Google describes Voice Access as a way to open apps, navigate and edit text hands-free, in English, Spanish, German, Italian, French, Portuguese and Japanese. It needs microphone and notification permission, and on recent versions you set up a shortcut to start it. It is aimed at controlling the whole device rather than at fast, polished dictation, so many people combine it with a voice keyboard.

Option 4: a floating dictation bubble

With a keyboard microphone you still have to reach the key. A floating bubble stays on screen where your thumb or finger rests, so one tap starts dictation in the app you are using. DictoKey offers this next to a standard voice keyboard; the two work independently, so if one is unavailable the other still works.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I type on Android with just one finger?

Yes. Gboard's glide typing lets you slide one finger across the letters of a word and lift it at the end, so you do not tap each key. Google notes that not all languages work with it. Voice typing goes further: you speak and the text appears without touching the keys.

How do I turn on glide typing in Gboard?

Google's help page says to open Settings, then System, Languages & input, Virtual keyboard and Gboard, and choose the Glide typing option. Menu names can differ on a custom Android version from your device manufacturer.

Can I dictate instead of typing with one finger?

Yes. Gboard has a microphone at the top of the keyboard, and DictoKey adds its own voice keyboard and a floating bubble you can move, resize and switch on app by app. DictoKey needs an internet connection because it transcribes in the cloud.

What is Voice Access?

Google describes Voice Access as a way to control your device by voice: open apps, navigate and edit text hands-free. Its help page lists English, Spanish, German, Italian, French, Portuguese and Japanese. It is a different tool from a dictation keyboard.

Is DictoKey free for one-handed use?

There is a free plan with a daily limit of 30 dictations and no account is needed. Premium costs about $7.50 per month with a 7-day free trial. DictoKey needs an internet connection because speech is transcribed in the cloud.
