Gboard Voice Typing Not Working? Fixes and Alternatives

Published October 20, 2026 · DictoKey Team · 6 min read

Short answer. When Gboard voice typing stops working or misses words, check three things first: the microphone permission for Gboard, whether your language is enabled and supported, and whether you are on a Pixel that needs Advanced voice typing instead. If it still fails, DictoKey is an alternative with its own voice keyboard and a floating bubble, with Clean mode adding punctuation automatically.

Gboard voice typing is free and built into most Android phones, so when the microphone does nothing, shows no “Speak now” prompt, or drops the first words of a sentence, it is frustrating. Most fixes are small. This page walks through them in order, using Google's own help pages, and only at the end explains where another app can help. If you already decided to switch, see our Gboard voice typing alternative comparison.

Fix 1: check the microphone permission

Google's Gboard help says that when you tap the microphone, you may be asked to choose a recording option: “While using app”, “Only this time” or “Don't allow”. “Don't allow” blocks recording. Google's Android help describes how to change this later:

  1. Open Settings and tap Apps.
  2. Tap Gboard (open the full app list if you do not see it).
  3. Tap Permissions, then Microphone.
  4. Select Allow or Allow only while using the app.

On recent Android versions you can also go to Settings > Security & privacy > Privacy and check that Microphone access is switched on. Menu names vary between brands and Android versions.

Fix 2: check the language

Google states that talk-to-text does not work with every language, so confirm that your language is supported and enabled. To switch language while dictating, tap the language abbreviation at the bottom left of the voice toolbar; Google notes that multiple languages must be enabled first. A mismatch between the language you speak and the language selected is a common reason for wrong or missing words.

Fix 3: on a Pixel, use Advanced voice typing

Google's help notes that some Pixel devices may need Advanced voice typing instead of the standard mode. According to Google, it requires a Pixel 6 or later, supports English, French, German, Italian, Japanese and Spanish, and adds automatic punctuation, emoji by voice and editing by voice commands. Google also says your primary Gboard language and device language must match: add the language in device settings and move it to the top of the list. If the feature is missing on a new Pixel, Google says updates can take a few hours, so connect to Wi-Fi, restart the phone and charge it overnight. On Android 12, Google advises updating the Android System Intelligence app through Google Play.

Fix 4: use voice commands for punctuation and corrections

Part of what feels like “not working” is punctuation. Standard Gboard voice typing expects you to say phrases such as “comma”, “question mark”, “new line” or “new paragraph”. To replace a single word, Google says to touch and hold it, tap the microphone and say the new word. To see the full list, tap Menu or the language abbreviation, then “Show voice commands”.

Other steps people try

Third-party troubleshooting articles also suggest turning off battery saver, force-stopping and reopening Gboard, clearing its cache or reinstalling Gboard updates. Google's own pages we read do not list these steps, so treat them as general Android housekeeping rather than documented fixes. They cost nothing, but they do not always work.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is Gboard voice typing not working on my Android phone?

The most common causes are a blocked microphone permission, a language that is not enabled or not supported for voice typing, or a missing update. Google's Gboard help says talk-to-text does not work with every language, so confirm yours is supported and enabled. Then check the microphone permission for Gboard in Settings, Apps, Permissions, Microphone.

How do I turn the Gboard microphone permission back on?

Open Settings, tap Apps, choose Gboard (use the full app list if you do not see it), tap Permissions, then tap Microphone and select Allow or Allow only while using the app. Menu names can differ slightly between phone brands and Android versions.

Why does Gboard voice typing miss or add words?

Google's help pages do not list a single cause. Practical things to check are the language selection, a steady connection, speaking in short phrases and, on a Pixel 6 or later, switching to Advanced voice typing, which Google describes as adding automatic punctuation and voice editing. If errors continue, a different dictation app may suit you better.

Does Gboard voice typing have a different mode on Pixel phones?

Yes. Google's help says some Pixel devices may need Advanced voice typing instead. It requires a Pixel 6 or later and supports English, French, German, Italian, Japanese and Spanish for voice typing at the time we checked.

Does DictoKey work offline if Gboard stops working?

No. DictoKey transcribes speech in the cloud and needs an internet connection. It has a free plan with a daily limit of 30 dictations, and it works with its own keyboard or a floating bubble, so it can serve as a backup when Gboard voice typing fails.
