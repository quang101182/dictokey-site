Best floating voice bubble apps for Android in 2026 (compared)

Published April 25, 2026 — Updated October 27, 2026

Short answer: a floating voice bubble lets you dictate into most apps without switching keyboards. On Android, DictoKey and Wispr Flow are the two bubble apps built for dictation; Voice Access and Floating Mic are free alternatives with fewer features. We build DictoKey, so weigh our ranking with that in mind.

Typing on a phone is slow. In a peer-reviewed study, speech input was about three times faster than a phone keyboard. A floating voice bubble removes the friction: it hovers above most apps, you tap it, speak, and the text lands in the field you were looking at. This guide compares what you can check yourself (how the bubble works, text insertion, languages, translation, price). We ran no lab benchmark, so there are no invented accuracy or latency figures.

Reviews

1. DictoKey

DictoKey is a two-part pill (language | mic) that sits above most apps. Tap the mic and speak; long-press to dictate then translate. Drag the bubble onto Translate, Correct or Emojis to rework text you already wrote, or step back like on a keyboard. You can adjust size and opacity, anchor its position, and turn it on or off app by app.

Speech recognition runs in the cloud, so you need an internet connection. Clean mode restores punctuation and capitals, and translation covers 51 languages, with an Interpreter Mode for spoken conversations (free users can try it 3 times a day). The bubble coexists with TalkBack, and DictoKey also works as a standard Android keyboard. It is GDPR compliant, stores no recordings and shows no ads; banking apps, password managers and crypto wallets are blocked automatically. Free plan: 30 dictations a day, no account. Premium: about $7.50/month or about $55 per year, with a 7-day free trial for new subscribers; 1,000 dictations a day, Interpreter Mode included.

Limits: Android only (no iOS, no Mac), and internet required. Spoken corrections (“replace X with Y”) work in the DictoKey keyboard; the bubble edits text through its panel.

Verdict: a complete bubble if you want dictation, translation and a keyboard fallback in one app.

2. Voice Access (Google)

Voice Access is Google's accessibility app for controlling an Android phone by voice. It is not a classic bubble, but it overlays voice controls on top of apps and can dictate into a focused field. It is free and made by Google. It is built for hands-free control, so it feels heavier than a tap-to-dictate bubble. See the DictoKey accessibility page for how DictoKey approaches the same needs.

Verdict: pick it if you need full hands-free control of the phone; for quick dictation a bubble is lighter.

3. Floating Mic

Floating Mic is a minimalist overlay that hands audio to the phone's own speech recognizer, then copies the result or inserts it into the focused field. Strengths: simple, free, no translation or rewriting to configure. Limits: accuracy and languages depend on the recognizer your phone ships with.

Verdict: a no-frills option for people who want only a mic button.

4. AssistiveTouch-style overlays

Apps in this family offer draggable shortcut menus. Voice is a side feature, usually passing audio to the system recognizer and copying the result to the clipboard. They suit people who want a general toolbox, not a dictation tool.

Verdict: use them for shortcuts; choose a dedicated bubble for dictation.

5. Speechnotes

Speechnotes is a dictation notepad. Its bubble opens its own dictation screen rather than inserting text into the app you are in, so you copy or share the result afterwards. It suits people who already use Speechnotes.

Verdict: good as a notepad companion, not a tap-anywhere bubble.

6. Otter.ai

Otter.ai is a meeting transcription service: recorder, searchable transcripts, summaries. It does not provide a floating overlay that inserts text into other apps, so it belongs to another category than dictation bubbles.

Verdict: use it for meetings, not for everyday dictation.

7. Wispr Flow

Wispr Flow lists dictation on Android on its pricing page, alongside Mac, Windows and iOS. Its Free plan shows “limited weekly use on mobile”; Pro is $15 per user per month, or $12 per month billed annually, and it advertises 100+ languages. Its Android setup asks for Microphone, Accessibility Service and Display over other apps, and its help center says offline devices cannot start dictation. Wispr is well known for cleaning up long-form dictation.

Choose DictoKey if you want 51-language translation, an Interpreter Mode, or a keyboard that works alongside the bubble. Choose Wispr if you already rely on it on desktop and want the same tool on your phone.

Verdict: a strong pick for long-form writing if its free weekly limit and Pro price suit you.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a floating voice bubble app?+
A floating voice bubble is a small, draggable microphone button that stays above most apps on Android. You tap it, speak, and the dictated text goes into the field you were typing in, without switching keyboards.
Do voice bubbles need the Accessibility permission?+
Most need the "Display over other apps" permission to draw the bubble, and often the Accessibility Service permission to insert text into other apps. DictoKey's Accessibility permission shows the bubble, inserts your dictated text and reads the active field on your phone (for Previous / Next and spacing); that text is sent only when you drop the bubble on Translate, Correct or Emojis, and it is not stored.
What is the difference between a voice bubble and a voice keyboard?+
A voice keyboard replaces your default keyboard and appears when a text field is focused. A bubble floats above most apps and can be tapped without changing keyboards. DictoKey offers both in one app: if one is not available, the other still works.
Is Wispr Flow available on Android, and is it free?+
Wispr's pricing page lists dictation on Android, and its Free plan shows limited weekly use on mobile; Pro is $15 per user per month, or $12 per month billed annually (checked on 2026-10-27). Its Android setup asks for Microphone, Accessibility Service and Display over other apps. DictoKey's free plan allows 30 dictations a day.
Does DictoKey work offline?+
No. DictoKey uses cloud speech recognition, so it needs an internet connection. Apps that hand audio to your phone's built-in recognizer may work offline for languages installed on the phone.
Can a voice bubble translate what I say?+
Some can. DictoKey offers real-time translation across 51 languages, plus an Interpreter Mode for spoken conversations between two languages; free users can try the Interpreter 3 times a day. For the other apps in this comparison we found no inline translation in the sources we checked.
