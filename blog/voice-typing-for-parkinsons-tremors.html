Voice Typing for Parkinson's and Tremors on Android

Published November 3, 2026 · DictoKey Team · 6 min read

Short answer. If a tremor makes small keys hard to hit, dictation can replace most of the tapping. On Android you can start free with the microphone in Gboard or with Google's Voice Access, which controls the phone by voice. DictoKey adds an adjustable floating bubble you can place where your thumb is steadiest, restores punctuation automatically and works in 51 languages, but it needs an internet connection.

Typing on glass asks for exactly what a tremor makes difficult: a steady finger and a precise tap. Speaking asks for something different, so it is a sensible thing to try. This page is practical rather than clinical. It covers what the main Android options do, what you can adjust on the phone itself, and the honest limits, including the fact that a softer voice can make dictation harder.

Step 1: try what Android already offers

Gboard voice typing

Google's help page describes the basic routine: tap a text field, tap the microphone at the top of the keyboard, and speak when you see “Speak now”. You can say punctuation such as “period”, “comma”, “question mark” or “new paragraph”. To replace a word, touch and hold it, tap the microphone and say the new word. Google also notes that talk-to-text does not work with all languages.

Voice Access

Voice Access is Google's accessibility service for controlling an Android device with spoken commands. Google's page lists English, Spanish, German, Italian, French, Portuguese and Japanese. On the current version you start it from a shortcut you choose in Settings > Accessibility > Voice Access (a floating button, a navigation bar button, volume keys or a gesture), or by tapping “Touch to start” on its notification. Its command list includes “Type” followed by your words, “Replace”, “Delete”, “Undo” and punctuation commands, and you can tap anything by saying “Show numbers” and then a number, or “Show grid”. That is useful when tapping a small target is the problem. Google warns that if the phone is awake and unlocked, anyone can control it with their voice, and that Voice Access can use offline language packs.

If your voice has become softer

Speech can change with Parkinson’s, and a quiet or unclear voice is harder for any speech recognition engine to transcribe. Hospitals describe speech therapy programs such as LSVT LOUD, delivered by speech-language pathologists, that target problems like softness and difficulty forming sounds clearly. We are not clinicians and cannot judge your voice, but a few practical habits apply whichever tool you use: dictate in a quiet room, hold the phone close, speak in short phrases and check the text before sending. Test two or three apps on a good day and a harder day before choosing one.

Phone settings that can help a tremor

Besides dictation, an Android phone has touch settings that matter when a finger jitters. A tremor-focused Android guide from a rehabilitation lab describes Touch & hold delay, which sets how long you must keep a finger down before a tap becomes a long press, so accidental long presses are less likely. The same guide describes a setting that ignores repeated accidental touches on one Samsung model, under a different name. Menu names vary by manufacturer and Android version, so look in Settings > Accessibility on your phone.

Who should pick what

  • Pick Gboard voice typing if you want the quickest free start and are happy tapping the microphone yourself.
  • Pick Voice Access if tapping at all is the problem and you want to control the whole phone by voice, or if you often have no connection and can install offline language packs. It lists seven languages.
  • Pick DictoKey if you want a large, repositionable bubble beside your own keyboard, automatic punctuation, rewriting or translation, or one of its 51 languages, on any Android 10+ phone with no account.
  • Nothing stops you combining them: many people keep Gboard for typing and add a bubble for dictation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can voice typing help if I have tremors and find the touchscreen hard to use?

It can reduce how much you have to type. With dictation, the microphone replaces most taps on small keys, so a tremor affects fewer steps. You still have to start dictation, which is why a large, movable button or a voice command to start helps. Results vary from person to person, so try the free built-in options first.

Does voice typing work if Parkinson's has made my voice softer?

It depends on the day and the app. Speech problems such as a soft voice are common enough that speech-language pathologists offer programs such as LSVT LOUD for them. A quieter voice can be harder for any speech recognition to transcribe, so hold the phone close, dictate in a quiet room and test several apps. A speech-language pathologist is the right person to advise on your voice.

What is the easiest way to start dictating with shaky hands?

The built-in route is the Gboard microphone: tap a text field, tap the microphone at the top of the keyboard and speak. Voice Access goes further and lets you control the phone by voice, including dictating with the Type command. DictoKey adds a floating bubble that you can enlarge and anchor where your thumb lands most reliably.

Does DictoKey work offline?

No. DictoKey transcribes speech in the cloud, so it needs an internet connection. Google says Voice Access can work without a network connection if you install offline speech recognition language packs, which is an advantage if you are often offline.
