What goes wrong when you type in several languages

Autocorrect is built around one language at a time, so it can rewrite a correct word in your second language. Switching layouts costs a tap or a long press on every change. Accented letters and non-Latin scripts add more friction. Speaking avoids some of this, because you choose a language once and say the sentence, but it needs a connection and is not always suitable in public.

Gboard: the free default

Google explains that you can add a keyboard language without changing your phone’s language: open Gboard settings, go to Languages, and add a language and layout. To change language you hold the space bar, or turn on the optional globe key. For voice, Google says talk-to-text does not work with all languages and that several languages must be enabled to switch the dictation language. The pages we opened do not give a total number of languages or a simultaneous-language limit, so we leave those as “—”.

Choose Gboard if you want a free, familiar keyboard that is already on your phone.

SwiftKey: made for typing in several languages

Microsoft states that SwiftKey on Android allows up to five languages simultaneously (two on iOS) and supports 700+ languages on Android. It estimates which enabled language you are using and adjusts predictions. For languages with the same alphabet you just type; for different alphabets you slide left or right on the space bar. If your daily problem is typed autocorrect across languages, this is the strongest documented option here.

DictoKey: speak, in 51 languages

DictoKey gives you two independent ways to dictate: a standard Android keyboard and a floating bubble above your apps. The bubble is a two-part pill, language on one side and microphone on the other. Tap the microphone to dictate; tap the language half to change the dictation and translation language, with an Auto-detect option in the language choice. Clean mode restores punctuation and capital letters, and a long press dictates then translates.

You can also drag the bubble onto Translate, Correct or Emojis to rework text already in the field. DictoKey works in most apps, blocks banking and password apps automatically, and transcribes in the cloud, so it needs internet. It covers 51 languages, fewer than the totals Microsoft gives for SwiftKey. For voice-first translation, see our guide to speaking one language and typing another.

Who should pick what

  • You type most of the time in two or more languages: SwiftKey, for its documented five-language support.
  • You want free and already installed: Gboard.
  • You speak more than you type, or write to people in another language: DictoKey, ideally next to your typing keyboard.
  • You have sore hands or tremors: voice can reduce typing; see Gboard voice typing alternatives and our accessibility page.
  • You need to dictate without internet: DictoKey is cloud-based, so it is not the one.

Microsoft says SwiftKey on Android allows up to five languages simultaneously, and two on iOS. The Gboard help pages we could open explain how to add languages and switch them (hold the space bar, or use the globe key) but do not state a simultaneous-language limit, so we do not give one.
