Repetitive strain injury (RSI) and tendon problems often come from the same pattern: many small, repeated movements. The NHS describes RSI as pain, aching, burning or throbbing that usually starts gradually and can affect the shoulders, elbows, forearms, wrists, hands and fingers, and lists typing among the repetitive activities that can be involved. Texting and tapping on a small screen is a lot of repetition, so cutting it down can matter. This page compares three ways to dictate on Android using official pages checked on November 9, 2026. We make DictoKey, so we also say plainly where the others fit better.

What voice typing can and cannot do

Speaking replaces many taps with one. That can help you type less across a day, especially for long messages, emails and notes. It cannot diagnose or treat anything. The NHS says to see a GP if symptoms are not going away or are getting worse, and notes that in many areas you can refer yourself to NHS community musculoskeletal services for help such as physiotherapy. Its self-help advice includes staying active, limiting activity at first and increasing it gradually, and talking to your employer about changing how you work. It does not mention voice software, so treat dictation as a practical way to reduce typing, not as medical advice.

Start with the simplest thing: Gboard’s microphone

According to Google’s help page, you open an app where you can type, tap the text field, tap the microphone at the top of the keyboard and, when you see “Speak now”, dictate. You can say period, comma, exclamation point, question mark, new line or new paragraph to insert punctuation, although these phrases may not be available in every language. Google also warns that talk-to-text does not work with all languages. The page does not say whether it works offline.

If this already works for you, there is no need to install anything. The next options matter when reaching the microphone is awkward, saying punctuation becomes tiring, or you want to avoid touching the screen at all.

Voice Access: when you want to avoid the screen entirely

Google describes Voice Access as a way to control your device with spoken commands, such as opening apps, navigating and editing text hands-free. On version 17.0 and later you turn it on under Settings > Accessibility > Voice Access by enabling its shortcut. To use it without a connection, you download a language for on-device speech recognition. It is a strong choice if you want to cut touch input beyond typing, though it takes longer to learn than a single microphone.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can voice typing help with RSI on Android?

Voice typing can reduce how much you type on a touchscreen, which is the repeated movement behind many RSI complaints. It is not a treatment, and it does not replace advice from a doctor or physiotherapist. If symptoms are not going away or are getting worse, the NHS advises seeing a GP.

Is there a free voice typing option for sore wrists?

Yes. Gboard has a built-in microphone and Voice Access is a Google app; we did not find a price on either official page. DictoKey has a free plan of 100 dictations a day in the first week, then 30 a day, with no account needed.

Can I dictate punctuation so I do not touch the keyboard?

With Gboard you can say words such as period, comma, question mark or new line, though this may not be available in every language. DictoKey’s Clean mode adds punctuation and capital letters automatically, so there is less to say and less to fix.

Does DictoKey work without internet?

No. DictoKey transcribes in the cloud and needs an internet connection. Google says Voice Access can work without a connection if you install offline language packs.

Can I edit text without typing?

In Gboard you can touch and hold a word, tap the microphone and say the replacement. In DictoKey you can drag the bubble onto Correct, Translate or Emojis to rework text you already wrote, or step back with Previous. Voice Access can also edit text by voice.

Is DictoKey safe to use for messages?

DictoKey is GDPR compliant, shows no ads and stores no recordings. It automatically blocks banking apps, password managers and crypto wallets, so it works in most apps but not in those sensitive ones.
