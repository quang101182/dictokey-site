Speak in One Language, Type in Another on Android

Published November 16, 2026 · DictoKey Team · 6 min read

Short answer. On Android you have two kinds of tools. Gboard types what you say in the language you pick, while Google Translate’s live translate and Samsung’s Interpreter translate speech on their own screens. DictoKey does both steps in one flow: you dictate in one of 51 languages and it can translate the result into the field you are writing in. It needs an internet connection.

Writing to a colleague, a landlord or a relative in a language you speak better than you type is a common need. This page compares four Android options, using Google and Samsung help pages checked on November 16, 2026. We make DictoKey, so we also say when another option is the better pick.

Two different jobs: translate a conversation or write a message

Translating a conversation and writing a translated message sound alike but need different tools. In a conversation, two people take turns and listen to or read each other’s translation. In a message, you want finished text sitting in a chat or an email, ready to send. Most tools are built for the first job; fewer put the translated words directly into the field you are typing in.

If you want to translate text you typed rather than text you spoke, our guide on translating text from your Android keyboard covers that case.

Option 1: Gboard voice typing

Google’s help page explains how to type with your voice and says talk-to-text does not work with all languages. To change the dictation language you need more than one keyboard language enabled, then you tap the language abbreviation in the voice toolbar and pick another. A second Google page says that on Pixel 6 and later, voice typing can detect the language you speak automatically in some languages, and you can turn that off under Auto-switch language.

That page does not describe translating speech as you go; it only links to a separate “Translate as you type” article, which we could not open when we checked, so we do not describe it. For free dictation in the language you speak, Gboard is a sensible default.

Option 2: Google Translate live translate

In the Translate app, you tap live translate, choose a mode and your two languages. Google lists four modes: Listening (through headphones), Conversation (translations play aloud while you take turns), Text only, and Face to face, where each speaker sees the transcription and translation for their own language on their half of the screen. Offline live translation covers only certain pairs with English, such as Spanish, French, German and Japanese, and Google says offline downloads are currently only available for Pixel 9, 10 and 11 users.

It is a strong choice for a face-to-face exchange with someone who does not share your language. The result stays in the Translate app, so you copy it across if you want it in a message.

Option 3: Samsung Interpreter and Live translate

Samsung describes Interpreter for conversations and Live translate for calls on select Galaxy phones and tablets with Galaxy AI. The page says it needs Android 14 with One UI 6.1 or higher. English and Spanish come preinstalled in the Interpreter app and other languages need a free download. Live translate also works in some third-party apps including WhatsApp, Telegram and Google Meet, and Samsung says only one person should use it at a time on a call.

If you own a supported Galaxy phone, it is already there and basic Galaxy AI features are free according to Samsung, although enhanced features may be subject to fees.

Option 4: dictate and translate with DictoKey

DictoKey gives you two independent ways to dictate: a standard Android keyboard and a floating bubble above your apps. The bubble is a two-part pill, language on one side and microphone on the other. Tap the microphone to dictate; a long press dictates and then translates (adjustable). Tap the language half to open a menu where you set the dictation language and the translation language, and swap them in one gesture.

Three more tools help when you already have text. Drag the bubble onto Translate, Correct or Emojis to rework what you wrote in the field. Select text in another app and choose Translate with DictoKey from Android’s selection menu to see the translation in a card, which you can copy or, when the app allows, use to replace the selection. And Interpreter Mode handles spoken conversations between two languages; free users can try it 3 times a day. There is also an auto-detect option in the language choice.

Clean mode restores punctuation and capital letters automatically, so the dictated sentence reads naturally before it is translated. Reread names, numbers and dates before sending, as with any machine translation.

A simple workflow for a translated message

  1. Open the chat or email where the message will go.
  2. Set the dictation language to the one you speak and the translation language to the one your reader uses.
  3. Dictate one or two sentences at a time; shorter chunks translate more reliably and are easier to check.
  4. Reread the translated text. If a phrase is wrong, correct it with the Correct action instead of retyping.
  5. For something important, ask a fluent speaker to read it before you send it.

Who should pick what

  • Pick Gboard voice typing if you only need free dictation in the language you speak and you do not need translation.
  • Pick Google Translate live translate if you are talking face to face with someone and want audio or a split screen, or you want offline pairs on a supported Pixel.
  • Pick Samsung Interpreter if you own a supported Galaxy phone and want translation built into calls and in-person conversations.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I speak one language and have it typed in another on Android?

Yes, with a tool that translates after it transcribes. Gboard types what you say in the language you pick, and Google Translate's live translate and Samsung's Interpreter translate speech inside their own screens. DictoKey dictates in one of 51 languages and can translate the result in the same flow: a long press on the microphone dictates and then translates (adjustable).

Does Gboard translate my voice into another language?

Google's voice typing help page describes typing in the language you select, and says multiple languages must be enabled to switch. It does not describe translating while you speak; it only links to a separate article called Translate as you type. We could not open that article, so we do not describe it here.

What is the difference between live translate and dictation with translation?

Live translate in Google Translate is built for two people taking turns, with audio, text-only and face-to-face modes. Dictation with translation puts the translated text into the field you are writing in, such as a message or an email, so you can send it.

Does DictoKey need internet to translate?

Yes. DictoKey transcribes in the cloud and needs a connection. Google says offline live translation in its Translate app covers only some language pairs with English and is currently available only on Pixel 9, 10 and 11.

Can I translate text I already wrote?

In DictoKey you can drag the bubble onto Translate to rework text already in the field, or select text in another app and choose Translate with DictoKey from Android's selection menu. The result appears in a card where you can copy it or, when the app allows, replace the selection.
