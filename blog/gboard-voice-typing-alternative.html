Gboard Voice Typing Alternative for Android

Published October 13, 2026 · DictoKey Team · 6 min read

Short answer. If Gboard's microphone is not enough, the main alternatives on Android are SwiftKey's built-in voice typing, the open-source FUTO Voice Input, Wispr Flow, and DictoKey. DictoKey adds a voice keyboard and a floating dictation bubble that works next to the keyboard you already use, restores punctuation automatically, and can rewrite or translate what you said, in 51 languages, without an account.

Gboard voice typing is the microphone at the top of Google's keyboard, and for many people it is perfectly good. Google's help page explains how to turn it on, and which spoken commands insert punctuation. People start looking elsewhere for a few concrete reasons: they want a different keyboard, they want punctuation handled for them, they want to rewrite or translate what they said, or the small microphone key is hard to hit reliably.

This page compares the realistic options and says plainly when staying with Gboard is the right call. Using a Galaxy phone? Read our separate Samsung voice typing alternative guide instead.

Step 1: get the most out of Gboard first

Before installing anything, check what Gboard already offers, according to Google's help pages:

  • Spoken punctuation. Say “period”, “comma”, “exclamation point”, “question mark”, “new line” or “new paragraph”. Google notes these phrases may not work in every language.
  • Advanced voice typing on Pixel. On Pixel 6 and later, punctuation is added automatically as you speak, and you can say the name of an emoji to insert it. Editing commands include “delete last word” and “clear all”. Google lists English, French, German, Italian, Japanese and Spanish for this mode, and says the text you speak stays on your device.
  • Pixel 8 and later. “Fix it” checks typos, grammar and punctuation, and detailed edits let you insert, delete, replace or capitalize words by voice. Google limits both to English (US).

If you own a recent Pixel and write in one of those languages, you may not need an alternative at all. The gaps appear on other phones and in other languages.

The alternatives, one by one

SwiftKey

Microsoft's support page says you dictate by tapping the microphone icon on the SwiftKey toolbar or long-pressing the comma key, and that your text appears as you speak while you can keep typing at the same time. The page also describes an older mode in which SwiftKey hands voice input to Google's keyboard; there, recognition languages and offline downloads are managed in the Google app. If you like SwiftKey's typing and prediction, its built-in microphone is the lowest-effort switch.

FUTO Voice Input

FUTO Voice Input is an Android app that adds speech-to-text to third-party keyboards and apps through Android's standard speech interfaces. Its code is public on GitHub, and AlternativeTo lists it as free, open source and able to work offline. Its authors say development has moved toward FUTO Keyboard, which has voice input built in. It is a strong choice if privacy on the device and open source matter most to you. Language coverage is limited to languages with enough training data, and compatibility depends on the keyboard you pair it with.

Wispr Flow

Wispr Flow's pricing page says dictation works on iOS and Android, as well as Mac and Windows. The free plan is capped at 1,000 words per week on mobile; Pro is $15 per month, or $12 per month billed yearly. It is a good fit if you already use it on a computer and want the same tool on your phone.

DictoKey

DictoKey gives you two independent ways to dictate. The first is a standard Android voice keyboard you can select in any text field, navigable with a screen reader. The second is a floating bubble that sits above most apps, so you can keep Gboard for typing and tap the bubble when you want to speak. If one is unavailable, the other still works.

The bubble is adjustable in size, opacity and anchored position, and you can turn it on app by app. It coexists with TalkBack instead of competing with the screen reader for control of the screen. Clean mode restores punctuation and capital letters, and AI rewriting and real-time translation work on what you dictated, across 51 languages. It runs on Android 10 and later, on any brand.

The honest limits: DictoKey needs an internet connection because speech is transcribed in the cloud, and the free plan is capped at 30 dictations per day. On privacy, it is GDPR compliant, stores no recordings, shows no ads, and blocks sensitive apps such as banking apps, password managers and crypto wallets automatically. Its Accessibility permission shows the bubble, inserts dictated text and reads the active field on the phone (for Previous / Next and spacing); that text is sent only when you choose Translate, Correct or Emojis, and it is not stored.

Who should pick what

  • Stay with Gboard if you have a recent Pixel and write in one of the languages Google lists for advanced voice typing: automatic punctuation and on-device speech are hard to beat there.
  • Pick SwiftKey if you want a different keyboard but the same quick tap-and-talk habit.
  • Pick FUTO Voice Input if you need open source or want to dictate without a connection, and your language is covered.
  • Pick Wispr Flow if you already use it on a computer and the free mobile word limit suits you.
  • Pick DictoKey if typing is painful, slow or unreliable for you, if you want punctuation, rewriting and translation handled for you, or if you want a bubble beside the keyboard you already use, with no account. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best Gboard voice typing alternative?

It depends on what bothers you. SwiftKey has its own microphone and keeps a similar typing layout. FUTO Voice Input is open source and works offline. Wispr Flow and DictoKey both add an AI layer on top of dictation. DictoKey is the one built accessibility-first: a voice keyboard plus a floating bubble, automatic punctuation, rewriting and translation, in 51 languages and without an account.

Can I use voice typing without Gboard?

Yes. SwiftKey has a microphone on its toolbar, FUTO Voice Input plugs into some third-party keyboards, and DictoKey offers its own voice keyboard as well as a floating bubble that sits above most apps while you keep whichever keyboard you prefer for typing.

Does Gboard voice typing work offline?

Google's Gboard help page does not say either way, so we cannot confirm it. Google's page for advanced voice typing on Pixel phones says the text you speak stays on your device. FUTO Voice Input is described as working offline. DictoKey does not work offline: it transcribes speech in the cloud and needs an internet connection.

Is DictoKey free?

DictoKey has a free plan with a daily limit of 30 dictations at the time of writing. Premium costs about $7.50 per month, or less on the annual plan, and both plans include a 7-day free trial for new subscribers. Google Play shows the exact price in your local currency.

Does DictoKey add punctuation automatically?

Yes. Its Clean mode restores punctuation and capital letters, so a dictated message usually does not need fixing. With Gboard you can say punctuation aloud, such as period, comma or question mark, and on Pixel 6 and later advanced voice typing adds punctuation automatically.

Who is DictoKey designed for?

DictoKey is a voice-to-text accessibility tool for people for whom typing on a touchscreen is painful, slow or unreliable: limited hand mobility, tremors and Parkinson's disease, arthritis, RSI and chronic pain, dyslexia and ADHD. It also suits anyone who writes faster by speaking.
