How SwiftKey voice typing works

According to Microsoft's support page for SwiftKey on Android:

  • Multimodal voice typing is enabled by default. You start it with the microphone icon on the toolbar, or by long-pressing the comma key.
  • It lets you dictate while the keyboard stays available for typing.
  • If you turn off Multi-modal voice typing under Settings, Rich input, SwiftKey uses Google's voice input. The Google app must be installed and updated, or voice typing may not work correctly.
  • Microsoft says it currently supports English only for voice to text.
  • Your voice typing data is sent to Microsoft's speech services to turn speech into text. Anonymized clips may be used to improve recognition; you can opt out with Stop contributing in the voice typing help settings.
  • Explicit language is blocked by default; you can change this in the voice typing settings after turning off multimodal voice typing.

Why people look beyond SwiftKey voice typing

  • Language. Microsoft documents English only for voice to text. Anyone who writes in another language, or switches between two, needs another tool.
  • It is tied to one keyboard. If you move to another keyboard, SwiftKey's microphone goes with SwiftKey.
  • Small targets. Starting dictation means finding a toolbar icon or a long-press on the comma key. For people with tremors or limited hand mobility, that precise touch is often the hard part.
  • Transcription only. Tidying a rambling message or translating it are separate jobs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does SwiftKey have voice typing?

Yes. Microsoft's support page says multimodal voice typing is enabled by default in SwiftKey on Android: tap the microphone icon on the toolbar or long-press the comma key to dictate while the keyboard stays visible. The same page says that, for now, SwiftKey supports English only for voice to text.

Can I use Google voice typing inside SwiftKey?

Yes. If you turn off Multi-modal voice typing under Settings, Rich input, SwiftKey uses Google's voice input instead. Microsoft notes that the Google app must be installed and updated, or voice typing may not work correctly.

Where does SwiftKey send my voice?

Microsoft's support page states that your voice typing data is sent to Microsoft's speech services to turn speech into text, and that anonymized voice clips may be used to improve recognition unless you choose Stop contributing in the voice typing help settings.

Do I have to give up SwiftKey to use DictoKey?

No. DictoKey has its own voice keyboard, but it also offers a floating dictation bubble that appears above most apps, so you can keep SwiftKey for typing and use the bubble only when you want to speak. Banking apps, password managers and crypto wallets are blocked automatically.

Is DictoKey free?

DictoKey has a free plan with a daily limit of 30 dictations at the time of writing. Premium costs about $7.50 per month, or about $55 per year, with a 7-day free trial for new subscribers; Google Play shows the exact price in your local currency.

Does DictoKey work offline?

No. DictoKey transcribes speech in the cloud and needs an internet connection.
