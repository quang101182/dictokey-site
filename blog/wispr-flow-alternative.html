Bubble only, or keyboard plus bubble?

Wispr Flow's bubble sits on top of your apps and leaves your current keyboard in place, which is convenient if you are happy with that keyboard.

DictoKey offers the same kind of floating bubble, and also a regular Android keyboard you can select in any text field. Having both is more than a convenience: they are independent. On devices where Android's Advanced Protection Mode is enabled, for example, Android blocks floating bubbles system-wide; DictoKey says so directly, and its voice keyboard stays fully available.

Accessibility: who benefits most

DictoKey is built as a voice-to-text accessibility tool for people for whom a touchscreen keyboard gets in the way of writing:

  • Motor impairments and limited hand mobility — when reaching small keys accurately is difficult.
  • Tremors and Parkinson's disease — when the finger does not land where it was aimed.
  • Arthritis, RSI and chronic pain — when repeated tapping hurts.
  • Dyslexia and ADHD — when spoken words come more easily than written ones.

For these readers, some rows of the table weigh more than they seem. No account means no sign-up form to type before the first dictation. Android 10+ matters for people who keep an older phone they are used to. The bubble can be adjusted in size, opacity and position to match your reach, and a button copies your last dictation again so you do not have to repeat it. The honest limits are on the same page: DictoKey needs an internet connection, and speech is transcribed on a server, then discarded.

When Wispr Flow may suit you better

  • Your language is not among DictoKey's 52. Wispr Flow announces more than 100 languages.
  • You want unlimited words on a free plan right now. Wispr's free plan on Android offers unlimited words for a limited time, whereas DictoKey's free plan is 30 dictations per day.
  • You only want a bubble, your phone runs Android 13 or later, and signing in to an account is not a problem for you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Wispr Flow available on Android?

Yes. Wispr Flow has been available on Android since February 23, 2026. It works through a floating bubble and an Android accessibility service rather than as a keyboard, so it runs alongside the keyboard you already use. Wispr's documentation lists Android 13 as the minimum version.

What is the main difference between Wispr Flow and DictoKey on Android?

Wispr Flow on Android is a floating bubble. DictoKey offers two independent ways to write by voice: an Android keyboard and a floating bubble that works in every app. DictoKey also works without an account and runs on Android 10 or later, while Wispr Flow requires signing in to an account and Android 13 or later.

Do Wispr Flow and DictoKey work offline?

No, neither does. Wispr Flow's Android requirements state that transcription needs an internet connection. DictoKey also transcribes in the cloud, so it needs an internet connection too; speech is transcribed on a server and then discarded.

How much does Wispr Flow cost compared with DictoKey?

Wispr Flow Pro is listed at $15 per month, or $12 per month billed annually, and its free plan offers unlimited words on Android for a limited time. DictoKey has a free plan with 30 dictations per day and a Premium plan with a 7-day free trial; the current Premium price is shown on Google Play.

Is there a limit on how long I can dictate?

Wispr Flow's Android documentation states that a dictation session lasts up to 5 minutes. DictoKey's free plan includes 30 dictations per day, and a Premium plan with a 7-day free trial is available; check the Google Play listing for the current plan details.

Who is DictoKey designed for?

DictoKey is a voice-to-text accessibility tool for people for whom typing on a touchscreen is painful, slow or unreliable: motor impairments and limited hand mobility, tremors and Parkinson's disease, arthritis, RSI and chronic pain, and dyslexia or ADHD. Details are on dictokey.com/accessibility.
