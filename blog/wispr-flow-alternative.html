The word limit, explained

Wispr's pricing page lists the Free plan at 2,000 words per week on desktop and 1,000 words per week on mobile, with unlimited words on Pro, Growth and Enterprise. Android used to be unlimited during the launch period, so articles written earlier may show something different. For a rough sense of scale, 1,000 words is a few long emails or about a dozen short messages a day for a week.

DictoKey counts differently. The free plan allows 30 dictations per day, whatever their length, so a few long dictations or many short ones both fit. Neither model is better for everyone: if you dictate many short messages, a daily count suits you; if you write rarely but at length, a weekly word total may be enough.

Bubble only, or keyboard plus bubble?

Wispr's own documentation states that Flow on Android is a floating bubble: you focus a text field, tap the bubble to start recording, then tap the checkmark to stop and insert your text. That keeps the keyboard you already like, which is a real advantage.

DictoKey gives you two independent ways to dictate: a standard Android keyboard that works with screen readers, and a floating bubble over most apps. If one is not available in a given situation, the other still works. The bubble can be resized, made more or less transparent, anchored where you can reach it and enabled app by app, and it coexists with TalkBack. The accessibility permission shows the bubble, inserts dictated text and reads the active field on the phone (for Previous / Next and spacing); that text is sent only when you choose Translate, Correct or Emojis, and it is not stored; it does not record keystrokes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a word limit on Wispr Flow for Android?

Wispr's pricing page lists the Free plan at 1,000 words per week on mobile (2,000 per week on desktop) and unlimited words on paid plans. Wispr's Android launch in February 2026 was described as free and unlimited for a limited time, so older articles may show a different limit. DictoKey's free plan counts dictations instead of words: 30 per day.

Does Wispr Flow work as a keyboard on Android?

No. According to Wispr's documentation, Wispr Flow on Android is a floating bubble that needs a focused text field and a visible on-screen keyboard. You tap the bubble to record and tap the checkmark to insert the text. DictoKey offers a standard Android keyboard and a floating bubble, and either one works if the other is unavailable.

What does Wispr Flow need on Android?

Wispr lists Android 13 or later, an internet connection (offline transcription is unavailable), microphone access, the display-over-other-apps permission and Accessibility access to insert dictated text, plus a sign-in. DictoKey runs on Android 10 and later, needs no account to start, and also needs an internet connection.

How do the prices of Wispr Flow and DictoKey compare?

Wispr lists Pro at $15 per user per month, or $12 per month billed annually. DictoKey Premium is 4.99 euros per month or 34.99 euros per year, with a 7-day free trial for new subscribers on both plans; Google Play shows the price in your local currency, about $5 per month. Both have a free tier: 1,000 words per week on Wispr mobile, 30 dictations per day on DictoKey.

Which apps does DictoKey work in?

No. DictoKey works in most apps, but it automatically blocks sensitive apps such as banks, password managers and crypto wallets. Wispr's documentation describes a similar behavior: its bubble is hidden in recognized banking and financial apps and in password fields.

Who should pick DictoKey instead of Wispr Flow?

People who want a real keyboard as well as a bubble, who would rather not create an account, who use a phone on Android 10, 11 or 12, or who prefer a free tier counted in dictations rather than weekly words. DictoKey is built accessibility-first for tremors, limited hand mobility, arthritis, RSI, dyslexia and ADHD, among others.
